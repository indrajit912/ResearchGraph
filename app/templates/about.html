The Story Behind ResearchGraph

Commitment to Public Data

ResearchGraph is built exclusively on publicly verifiable information. The vertices and edges in our network represent data aggregated from public academic records, pre-prints (such as arXiv), and public institutional directories. We strictly prohibit the submission or storage of any private, confidential, or unlisted information.

Around September 2026, I created a collaboration network feature for my personal website (see it here). The goal was simple: to display my research collaborators through a visually appealing, interactive force-directed graph.

After sharing the feature with my colleagues and collaborators, I realized that many other researchers also wanted a similar network on their own academic pages.

The Problem

Many brilliant researchers are not programmers. They do not maintain web applications, they do not want to manage databases, they have no time to configure complex D3.js graph visualization libraries, and they certainly do not want to deploy and host their own web infrastructure just to show a network graph.

Therefore, I decided to create ResearchGraph — a standalone service that provides researchers with a permanent URL to their interactive collaboration network.

How It Works

1
Register an account and verify your email.
2
Find yourself in the master database and claim your profile.
3
Crowdsource connections by adding missing colleagues to the global graph.
4
Share your permanent /r/your-name link on your personal website.

To prevent chaos, the database of researchers (the vertices) is strictly curated by moderators. However, the collaborations (the edges) are openly crowdsourced by the community.
